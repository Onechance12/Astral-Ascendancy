# Genre Research Notes

This document captures what Astral Ascendancy should learn from adjacent games. The goal is not to copy them. The goal is to understand what works and turn those lessons into a stronger, browser-native tactical card game.

## Games Reviewed

- MARVEL SNAP
- Faeria
- Duelyst
- Stormbound
- Slay the Spire
- GWENT
- Legends of Runeterra
- Into the Breach
- Hearthstone, as a presentation reference

## MARVEL SNAP

Reference: https://marvelsnap.helpshift.com/hc/en/3-marvel-snap/faq/93-what-are-locations/

What works:

- Locations change the match without adding many player inputs.
- Revealed location effects create drama and adaptation.
- Short matches make experimentation easy.
- Random board modifiers create memorable stories.

What Astral should learn:

- Use world sectors to create match variety.
- Make the board reveal and transform over time.
- Keep the early turn count fast enough that a bad draw does not feel like a 30-minute punishment.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not make board effects so random that strategy feels fake.
- Do not hide too much information. A tactical board needs readable plans.

Astral translation:

- Every match starts with several neutral sectors.
- World cards and cosmic events reveal or alter sectors.
- Center anomaly can create SNAP-like drama, but the player should still have agency through movement, structures, and card choices.

## Faeria

Reference: https://www.faeria.com/the-hub/guide/61-faeria-academy-chapter-2-land-placement

What works:

- Card game plus living board gives identity.
- Players build the battlefield while playing.
- Land placement creates rush, midrange, and control strategies.
- Resource wells make board control economically meaningful.

What Astral should learn:

- The board should be developed, not merely occupied.
- Terrain and resource control should matter as much as raw attack.
- A simple board tutorial is required because land/terrain games can feel intimidating.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not let land placement become too slow or hard to parse.
- Avoid forcing players to count too many hidden requirements before they can play a fun card.

Astral translation:

- World cards terraform sectors.
- Structures are built on controlled worlds.
- Resource nodes in the 5x5 board create extra Resonance, Influence, and material.
- Campaign and domain rewards can become playable world cards.

## Duelyst

Reference: https://duelyst.fandom.com/wiki/Getting_Started

What works:

- Tactical grid plus card game is instantly distinct.
- Commanders/generals give identity and a visible target.
- Minions, spells, artifacts, and board positioning create varied decisions.
- Central mana springs reward movement and map control.
- Factions help players pick a style.

What Astral should learn:

- A commander should be present, visible, and tactically relevant.
- Resource bonuses in the center create natural conflict.
- Movement and adjacency must stay clear.

What Astral should avoid:

- A huge board can overwhelm new players if every piece has many options.
- Tactical card games need strong visual highlights for legal moves and attacks.

Astral translation:

- Use a smaller 5x5 board instead of 9x5.
- Place commanders outside the grid as battlefield presences, not movable kings.
- Put important resource/anomaly pressure near the center row.

## Stormbound

Reference: https://stormbound-kitty.com/guides/battle

What works:

- Front line matters.
- Board presence and board control are separate but connected decisions.
- Structures are high-risk, high-reward because they need to survive.
- Simple lanes can still create deep pressure.

What Astral should learn:

- Placement should create pressure, not just stats.
- Structures should generate value over time but cost tempo.
- The player should constantly decide between advancing, clearing, building, or developing.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not make structures automatic value engines with no placement risk.
- Do not let forward pressure become unreadable.

Astral translation:

- Controlled sectors determine where worlds and structures can be played.
- Structures cannot attack and usually do nothing immediately.
- If protected, they produce resources, shields, portals, drones, spores, or damage.

## Slay the Spire

Reference: https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/slay-the-spire/id6448808007

What works:

- Every run feels different.
- Characters have identity.
- Cards, relics, and events create combo discovery.
- Small rewards after every fight keep momentum high.

What Astral should learn:

- Campaign should support roguelite-style runs later.
- Commanders should have unlockable relics, traits, and world modifiers.
- The player should constantly receive interesting card/resource choices.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not make deck growth so random that constructed collection identity disappears.
- Do not overload the first alpha with full roguelite systems.

Astral translation:

- Campaign planets can offer cards, relics, world cards, crew, or structures.
- Commander progression unlocks alternate starting cards and passive powers.

## GWENT

Reference: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.cdprojektred.gwent

What works:

- Cards and battlefields have strong art presentation.
- Rows create positional strategy without a huge grid.
- Round structure creates bluffing and resource tension.
- The game sells fantasy through card art and board ambience.

What Astral should learn:

- Presentation is not polish later. It is product identity.
- Board zones should be clear at a glance.
- Cards need dramatic art, VFX, and readable rarity.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not make the game only about numbers if the fantasy is galactic war.
- Avoid too much UI chrome around the battle.

Astral translation:

- Every 5x5 sector should have a visible state: empty, controlled, world, structure, hazard, portal, corrupted, fortified.
- Faction board skins and card VFX matter early.

## Legends of Runeterra

Reference: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.riotgames.legendsofruneterra&hl=en_US

What works:

- Champions anchor identity.
- Character progression gives long-term goals.
- Runs add powers, relics, and evolutions.
- Different champions feel meaningfully different.

What Astral should learn:

- Commanders should be collectible, upgradeable, and strategically distinct.
- Commander powers should push faction decks in different directions.
- Progression should unlock new play patterns, not only bigger numbers.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not let permanent upgrades break competitive fairness.
- Keep PvP separate from campaign power creep if campaign upgrades become large.

Astral translation:

- Commanders have campaign progression and cosmetic evolution.
- Ranked/fair PvP uses normalized commander loadouts.
- Campaign allows bigger, wilder commander powers.

## Into the Breach

Reference: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach

What works:

- Compact grid, deep tactics.
- Enemy intent is telegraphed.
- Positioning often matters more than raw damage.
- Environmental hazards create puzzle-like turns.

What Astral should learn:

- A 5x5 board can be deep if enemy intent, hazards, movement, and pushes are readable.
- Telegraphing attacks can make tactical decisions feel smart instead of random.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not make every turn a perfect puzzle. Card games need surprise and emotional swings.

Astral translation:

- AI enemies should telegraph intended attacks in campaign.
- World hazards should show next-turn pulses.
- Big attacks should draw lines before resolving.

## Hearthstone

Reference: presentation benchmark, not a mechanical template.

What works:

- Tactile board.
- Card drag feels physical.
- Attacks have punch.
- Packs feel ceremonial.
- The board is playful, interactive, and reactive.

What Astral should learn:

- Every click should have feedback.
- Pack opening must feel valuable even for common cards.
- The battle scene should have a sense of table, space, and impact.

What Astral should avoid:

- Do not become a simple lane battler with no spatial identity.
- Do not copy fantasy tavern tone. Astral is cosmic alien war.

Astral translation:

- PixiJS card physics, glow, attack lines, camera hit shake, particles, and audio hooks are core alpha work.

## Final Design Lessons

Astral should become:

- Smaller than Duelyst, deeper than a 3-lane prototype.
- More board-building than Hearthstone.
- More readable than a heavy tactics game.
- More animated and tactile than a dashboard.
- More world-driven than a normal CCG.
- Generous and free first, monetized later only after trust exists.

The core design target:

5x5 tactical card combat where players terraform the board with world cards, protect structures for resources, evolve living cards, and win through conquest, influence, or faction-specific ascendancy.
